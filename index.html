Overview

The story in seven steps

The Fitness Chain sells one brand through three locations that each keep their own records. This report reads 2,700 class sessions to show what that costs. Each step opens its own chapter.

  1. The setup. Same brand and dues, but seats filled range from 72.2% to 56.7% across locations.
  2. The surprise. Spin is the fullest class (70.9%) yet earns the least per session ($6.65).
  3. The loss. 4,537 cross-location requests were denied, 52.7% of them in rooms with empty seats.
  4. The why. Record errors run 4.0%, 11.1% and 17.7% by location, and errors cost 0.54 rating points.
  5. The consequence. Churn risk rises from 13.6% to 18.5% and no single location can see it.
  6. The people. Teaching load is uneven (3.0x) and unrelated to demand.
  7. The takeaway. The problem is visibility and access, not demand.

Data note: the 2,700 sessions are synthetic, generated using Google Colab from 16 sourced rules (see the DPAD deck, section 4). The match of tools to locations (LOC01 scheduling app, LOC02 shared spreadsheet, LOC03 manual log) is a modeling assumption.

Chapter 1 · The setup

Same brand, three very different rooms

Same logo, same dues, same class menu. Yet LOC01 fills 72.2% of its seats, LOC02 63.4% and LOC03 only 56.7%. Each location runs its own tool, so nobody sees the three side by side.

Chapter 3 · The loss

Members turned away while seats sat empty

A membership only works at its home location. Members from another location who asked for a class were refused 4,537 times in six months, about 1.68 per session. More than half of those refusals happened in rooms at least a quarter empty.

Figure 3.1 Denied requests by location
Split by whether the session still had 25% or more of its seats empty.
Figure 3.2 Denied requests per session
Peak sessions attract the most requests.
Finding. 2,392 denied requests (52.7%) landed on sessions with a quarter or more of the seats empty, and 4,048 (89.2%) could have been seated in that same session.Across all sessions 28,053 seats went unused.

Chapter 4 · The why

Three tools, three levels of record quality

The record error rate climbs from 4.0% at LOC01 to 11.1% at LOC02 and 17.7% at LOC03, in the order of how manual each tool is. No-shows follow the same ladder: 9.7%, 15.6% and 21.1% of booked seats stay empty.

Figure 4.1 Record error rate and no-show rate
Share of sessions with a record error, and share of bookings that never show up.
Figure 4.2 Average rating by record status
Scale 1 to 5. The axis starts at 3.0 to show the gap.
Finding. A session with a record error averages 4.04 stars against 4.57 without one, a gap of 0.54.Bad records are not only a back-office problem. Members feel them at the door.

Chapter 5 · The consequence

Churn risk climbs, and no one location can see it

Attendance per session fell from 20.6 in January to 17.0 in June (17.4% lower). Over the same months the share of attendees who barely visit rose from 13.6% to 18.5%. Each location only sees its own slice.

Finding. Sessions with a record error carry 25.7% churn-risk attendees against 14.8% without.7,941 attendees in total are flagged. That is the churn signal leadership cannot see today.

Chapter 6 · The people

Who carries the teaching load?

INS11 taught 271 sessions while INS05 taught 90, a 3.0x gap. Teaching more does not mean teaching fuller rooms: across the 15 instructors the correlation between sessions taught and class utilization is -0.05.

Chapter 7 · The takeaway

The problem is visibility and access, not demand

Put the six chapters together and one story remains.

What we saw

Peak rooms at 79.7% and off-peak rooms at 48.6%. 4,537 members refused at the door. Record errors in 10.9% of sessions, worst where the tool is most manual.

Why it matters

Errors cost ratings (4.04 vs 4.57) and raise churn risk (25.7% vs 14.8%). Churn risk grows from 13.6% to 18.5% while every location sees only its own slice.

What we decide next

Which way of sharing booking and reporting data across the three locations closes these gaps, while managers keep control of local scheduling.
